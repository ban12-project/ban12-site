Effective October 1, 2025, China introduces the 'K-Visa' to attract global innovation talent in STEM fields, similar to the 'H-1B' style but sponsorship-free.

## Policy Background & Definition
- Effective Date: October 1, 2025
- Establishment Basis: Revised 'Regulations on the Administration of Entry and Exit of Foreigners'.
- Core Positioning: Specifically for young foreign professionals in Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM).
- Strategic Significance: Sponsorship-free entry, allowing talent to enter first and then find jobs or start businesses.

## Eligibility Criteria
- **Education**: Bachelor’s degree or higher in STEM fields from recognized universities or research institutions.
- **Career Status**: Young professionals engaged in STEM education, research, or technology.
- **Fields**: Software Engineering, AI, Robotics, Data Science, Advanced Manufacturing, Semiconductors, etc.
- **Age Limit**: Targeting 'Young' professionals, typically early to mid-career (approx. 20s-30s).

## Key Benefits
- **Zero Sponsorship**: No need for an employer's invitation letter or job offer at the application stage.
- **Activity Freedom**: Free to work, research, intern, exchange academically, or engage in entrepreneurship.
- **Flexibility**: Allowing change of employers, multiple jobs, or freelancing. Longer stay periods and multiple entry rights.
- **Incentives**: Potential housing subsidies, relocation allowances, or tax benefits in some regions.

## Application Process & Materials
Apply via the 'New COVA System' (China Online Visa Application).

- **STEM Qualifications**: Degree certificates, credential verification.
- **Professional Ability**: CV, research achievements, patents, or publications.
- **Basic Documents**: Passport (6+ months validity), Photo (48mm×33mm), Residence Proof.
- **Others**: Health Check, Proof of Financial Capability.

## Fees
- US Citizens: ~$140 (Standard)
- Non-US Citizens: ~$23-68 (varies by entry count)
- Expedited Service: +$25
